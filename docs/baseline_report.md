## 1. Executive Summary & Protocol Specification

The pre-registered baseline-of-record is **FacePhys-Release** executed under **Path 3 (GT-timestamp timing)** with strict sequential determinism (`synchronous=True`).

### 1.1 Mathematical & Signal Processing Pipeline
1. **Model Backbone:** `rppg.Model('FacePhys.rlap')` — Continuous-time Controlled Differential Equation (CDE) neural state-space model for remote photoplethysmography.
2. **Timing & Alignment (Path 3):** Ground-truth hardware capture timestamps $t_{\text{GT}}[i]$ are passed directly to `update_frame(frame, timestamp=t_i)`. This anchors internal state evolution to true wall-clock time and allows the library watchdog to forward-fill acquisition latency.
3. **Bandpass Filtering:** 3rd-order zero-phase Butterworth bandpass filter spanning the physiological bandwidth $0.7 - 3.0\text{ Hz}$ ($42.0 - 180.0\text{ BPM}$).
4. **Spectral HR Estimation:** Welch Periodogram calculated via `scipy.signal.welch` with an FFT length $N_{\text{FFT}} = 16384$, Hann windowing, and $50\%$ segment overlap. Peak frequency $f_{\text{peak}} = \arg\max_{f \in [0.7, 3.0]} S_{xx}(f)$ is converted to $\text{HR} = f_{\text{peak}} \times 60.0\text{ BPM}$.
5. **Windowed Metrics:** 30.0-second sliding windows with a 1.0-second stride.
6. **Correlation & Physiological Lag Search:** Pearson correlation $r$ evaluated directly and over a strictly bounded physiological lag window of $\pm 0.5\text{ seconds}$ ($\pm 15$ frames at 30 Hz).
7. **SNR Calculation:** Ratio of in-band pulse harmonic power ($\pm 0.1\text{ Hz}$ surrounding $f_{\text{ref}}$ and $2 f_{\text{ref}}$) to residual noise power in the $[0.7, 3.0]\text{ Hz}$ band, reported in decibels ($\text{dB}$).

## 3. Physical Resolution of Path 2 Frequency Scaling

### 3.1 The Mystery of the Frequency Shift (107.01 vs 109.75 BPM)
In earlier evaluations, when video frames were fed using uniform synthetic timestamps $t_i = i / 30.0$ (Path 2), the estimated HR on `subject1` shifted systematically from $107.01\text{ BPM}$ to $109.75\text{ BPM}$ (+2.74 BPM error). An initial hypothesis conjectured that this discrepancy arose from dropped webcam frames.

### 3.2 Hardware Clock Frequency Mismatch Demonstration
A systematic examination of the ground-truth hardware timestamps $t_{\text{GT}}$ reveals the physical reality:
The webcam did not drop frames intermittently. Rather, the hardware acquisition thread ran at a nominal frame rate lower than 30.0 fps:
$$f_{\text{true}} = \frac{N_{\text{frames}} - 1}{t_{\text{GT}}[-1] - t_{\text{GT}}[0]}$$

Across the 7 subjects, the true capture frame rates are:
- `subject1`: $N = 1547$, $\Delta t = 52.691\text{ s} \implies f_{\text{true}} = \mathbf{29.3409\text{ fps}}$ (Ratio $30.0 / f_{\text{true}} = \mathbf{1.02246}$, $+2.25\%$)
- `subject3`: $N = 1801$, $\Delta t = 60.929\text{ s} \implies f_{\text{true}} = \mathbf{29.5426\text{ fps}}$ (Ratio $30.0 / f_{\text{true}} = \mathbf{1.01548}$, $+1.55\%$)
- `subject4`: $N = 1368$, $\Delta t = 46.234\text{ s} \implies f_{\text{true}} = \mathbf{29.5670\text{ fps}}$ (Ratio $30.0 / f_{\text{true}} = \mathbf{1.01465}$, $+1.46\%$)
- `subject5`: $N = 1550$, $\Delta t = 52.368\text{ s} \implies f_{\text{true}} = \mathbf{29.5791\text{ fps}}$ (Ratio $30.0 / f_{\text{true}} = \mathbf{1.01423}$, $+1.42\%$)
- `subject8`: $N = 2022$, $\Delta t = 67.638\text{ s} \implies f_{\text{true}} = \mathbf{29.8797\text{ fps}}$ (Ratio $30.0 / f_{\text{true}} = \mathbf{1.00403}$, $+0.40\%$)
- `subject9`: $N = 2016$, $\Delta t = 68.256\text{ s} \implies f_{\text{true}} = \mathbf{29.5212\text{ fps}}$ (Ratio $30.0 / f_{\text{true}} = \mathbf{1.01622}$, $+1.62\%$)
- `subject10`: $N = 2024$, $\Delta t = 67.716\text{ s} \implies f_{\text{true}} = \mathbf{29.8748\text{ fps}}$ (Ratio $30.0 / f_{\text{true}} = \mathbf{1.00419}$, $+0.42\%$)

When Path 2 assigns synthetic timestamps $t_i = i / 30.0$, the entire duration is artificially compressed by the factor $\frac{30.0}{f_{\text{true}}}$. In the Fourier domain, this dilation scales all temporal frequencies:
$$f_{\text{synthetic}} = f_{\text{phys}} \times \frac{30.0}{f_{\text{true}}}$$
For `subject1`:
$$107.0068\text{ BPM} \times 1.02246 = \mathbf{109.41\text{ BPM}}$$
Welch spectrum binning with finite windowing on the resampled 30 Hz grid yields an observed peak at $109.75\text{ BPM}$. 

**Conclusion:** The frequency shift is an artifact of interpreting a $29.34\text{ fps}$ physical recording as if it were recorded at $30.00\text{ fps}$. Path 3 (feeding true hardware timestamps) grounds the signal to real physical seconds and completely cures this frequency dilation.

## 4. Controlled Padding Sweep on Subject 4

`subject4` in UBFC is an anomaly: while all other subjects are uncropped full frames ($640 \times 480$), `subject4` was natively pre-cropped tightly to the face ($242 \times 242$), causing face contours to touch the image boundary.

To rigorously understand the spatial sensitivity of FacePhys without cherry-picking, a controlled padding sweep was executed across $0, 30, 60,$ and $90\text{ px}$ symmetric reflect padding (`cv2.BORDER_REFLECT`) under frozen baseline-v1 settings.
